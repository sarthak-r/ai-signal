Robynn: agents that edit your site through a propose-stage-measure loopProduct Hunt · this week

Robynn AI connects to a live site with no rebuild, recognises the CMS from the URL, then runs agents that audit every page against your brand, ideal customer, competitors and target keywords. A marketer points at any element, describes a change in plain language, and the agents rewrite and stage it; nothing goes live until a human approves. After publishing, it measures the change in Google Analytics, reinforces what moved the numbers and rolls back regressions.

The closed loop is the pattern worth copying: propose, stage for human approval, publish, measure, then reinforce or auto-revert. That's a safe shape for letting agents touch anything a customer sees. For a wealthtech product it maps onto agent-drafted client content or advice components — stage every change, require sign-off, measure the effect, and keep it reversible — which is roughly what a compliance reviewer would ask for anyway.

Build Patterns

A benchmark built the way you actually ship — one requirement at a time — and what it exposes about running agents unattended.

SlopCodeBench: models still can't build one issue at a time unattendedgithub.com/humanlayer · 27 Jul

HumanLayer published SlopCodeBench, which grades a model on maintaining code quality across a sequence of checkpoints rather than a single up-front spec — closer to how software is built, where you discover problems as you go. On the tested subset Opus 5 passed 24% of checkpoints strictly (4 of 17), left 8–11 bugs per problem at completion, and wrote 29,065 source lines at a 51% test-to-code ratio, against 11% for Opus 4.8 and roughly a third of the line count from other models. The authors' conclusion: for real-shaped work built one issue at a time, today's models can't run lights-off without steering.

This benchmark matches how you actually ship — requirements arrive incrementally — and it quantifies what teams feel: hand an agent a growing codebase unattended and it accumulates defects and code bloat. Scope agents to one issue at a time, keep a human at each checkpoint, and read a sudden 3x jump in lines or tests as a smell rather than throughput. It's the counterweight to the "let it run overnight" pitch, with numbers.
